# Stage 1: Map, understand the problem

Goal: know what the human wants to achieve, who has the problem, and where
the biggest opportunity is, then choose one target to focus on.

## Steps

1. **Long-term goal.** Where should this be in two years, if it goes well?
   One sentence, optimistic and concrete.
2. **Who.** The people with the problem, and anyone else who matters
   (who pays, who approves, who is affected). One concrete primary user.
3. **Questions that could sink it.** Turn the goal around: why could it
   fail? Each risk becomes a question ("Will freelancers trust an AI with
   their invoices?"). These are the questions discovery and the prototype
   must answer.
4. **Journey.** The primary user's path from their starting point to the
   outcome they want, in 5 to 12 steps, as it happens **today**. Mark where
   it hurts, where it is slow, and where they give up: end that step with
   `Pain:` and one word for the kind, then the detail (the template shows
   the form; the app draws the journey from it).
5. **Target.** One moment in the journey to focus on: the step where a
   solution would matter most and the riskiest question can be answered.
   Recommend one and say why. Write it as `Step N:` with its journey number,
   so the app can point at it.

Ask progressively, one round at a time. Challenge vague words ("everyone",
"easy", "fast"): ask who exactly, and what fast means. Summarise your
understanding before writing, and ask the human to confirm or correct it.

**Specialists:** the market researcher when the human cannot say who else
solves this today, or wants to know; customer research when interviews
exist or are planned; counsel when the concern scan finds money, personal
data, health, children or a regulated trade.

## Readiness checklist

**core**
- [ ] The long-term goal in one sentence
- [ ] The primary user, and the people around them who matter
- [ ] The journey as it happens today, with where it hurts
- [ ] At least three questions that could sink the idea
- [ ] One target in the journey, chosen by the human, with the reason

**thorough**
- [ ] What the user does today instead, and why that is not good enough, with evidence
- [ ] Every stakeholder: who pays, who approves, who is affected
- [ ] Every step of the journey marked as fact or assumption
- [ ] Which question is the riskiest, and what answer would stop the project
- [ ] Existing research read and cited, or the decision to do none
- [ ] Concerns from the concern scan (privacy, money, compliance, safety) placed on the journey

## Done when

The human confirms the problem, the journey and the target.
